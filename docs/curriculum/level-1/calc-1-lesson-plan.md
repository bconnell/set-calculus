## Embedded Resolver Architecture

### Identity Resolver

The Identity Resolver is used throughout the course to ask:

> What remains the same?

Working form:

```text
R_I(x) → invariant identity / relationship
```

It is exercised through equivalence, invariance, continuity, inverse relationships, reflection, projection, and transformation. The resolver is deliberately used before it is formally named and generalized in Lesson 28.

### Monad Resolver

The Monad Resolver is used throughout the course to ask:

> What singular resolution represents this state?

Working form:

```text
R_M(S) → singular resolved representation
```

It teaches movement from a possibility or relational state toward singular resolution while preserving the distinction:

```text
Potential != Resolved
```

The resolver is formally named and generalized in Lesson 29.

### Resolver Retrospective

Lesson 30 revisits the course after both resolver abstractions have been exposed. Students trace where they have already used each resolver in earlier calculus work.

The retrospective exposes the recurring architecture:

```text
Relation
  → Transform
  → Identity
  → Resolution
```

The pedagogical principle is to accumulate concrete operational experience before resolving that experience into the higher-level abstraction.
