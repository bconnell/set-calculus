# Bidirectional Temporal Reconciliation

## Philosophy Anchor

Set Calculus treats temporal resolution as relational rather than strictly sequential.

Unless a reconciliation boundary has been enacted, information may continue to affect the interpretation of connected states in more than one temporal direction.

## Bidirectional Resolution

A later state may change the interpretation of an earlier state, while an earlier state constrains what a later state can validly become.

```text
past state <-> present state <-> possible future
```

Resolution therefore propagates through relationships rather than only along the direction in which events were experienced.

A change in one connected state may require reconsideration of other connected states.

## Simultaneous Resolution

Resolution need not be understood as a sequence of isolated corrections.

When a connected structure reconciles, the meaning of the relationship may resolve across the connected set as a whole.

```text
A <-> B <-> C

new relation discovered at C
        ↓

A <-> B <-> C
reconciled as a connected structure
```

"Simultaneous" here describes the logical state of the resolved relationship.

It does not require the human observer to experience that reconciliation at the same moment.

## Lived-Timeline Divergence

A lived timeline is locally experienced.

```text
t1 -> t2 -> t3 -> t4
```

The resolver operating within that timeline may therefore temporarily carry a representation that no longer reflects all available relationships.

```text
available relational state:
t1 <-> t2 <-> t3

lived resolution:
t1 -> t2 -------->
          \
           unresolved divergence
```

This creates a temporary divergence between the currently available relational structure and the state presently carried by the observer.

Such divergence is not necessarily failure.

```text
divergence
!=
contradiction

divergence
=
temporarily unreconciled temporal state
```

## Resolver Catch-Up

Resolver catch-up occurs when previously available, newly available, or newly connected information becomes reconciled into the lived state.

```text
prior lived resolution
+
unresolved relationships
+
new or newly recognized information
        ↓
reconciliation
        ↓
updated lived resolution
```

A resolver catch-up may therefore produce understanding that was not available to conscious resolution earlier.

The philosophy allows for experiences in which an unresolved state remains structurally active until conditions permit its reconciliation.

```text
UNRESOLVED(t1)
        ↓
continued relational availability
        ↓
resolver catch-up
        ↓
RESOLVED(t2)
```

The important distinction is:

```text
reality may be relationally reconciled
before
the observer's lived model has reconciled
```

## Closure as a Reconciliation Boundary

Closure is not merely the declaration that something is finished.

Closure establishes a boundary across which some further reconciliation is no longer permitted to propagate automatically.

Without closure:

```text
A <-> B <-> C
```

A change at `C` may require reconsideration of `B`, and a changed `B` may require reconsideration of `A`.

With closure:

```text
A <-> B |CLOSURE| C
```

the system records that a reconciliation boundary exists.

Closure must therefore be explicit and traceable.

It should identify, where relevant:

```text
what is closed
what relationship is bounded
which direction or directions are bounded
why closure was enacted
what authority enacted it
what state existed at closure
whether closure is reversible
```

Closure does not erase prior relationships or provenance.

It constrains future reconciliation across the declared boundary.

## Temporal Resolution States

A useful philosophical distinction is:

```text
OPEN

relationships remain available
for continued reconciliation
```

```text
DIVERGED

the lived resolver has not yet reconciled
with the available relational structure
```

```text
RECONCILED

the currently available relationships
have been incorporated into the resolved state
```

```text
CLOSED

a declared boundary limits
further reconciliation across part of the structure
```

These states may change over time.

A previously reconciled state may diverge when new information appears.

A diverged state may later reconcile.

A closed boundary may remain closed or be reopened when its governing rules permit.

## Core Principle

```text
historical sequence
!=
resolution direction
```

Events may be lived sequentially while their relationships remain capable of bidirectional reconciliation.

The philosophy of Set Calculus therefore preserves enough state, relationship, context, and provenance to allow later knowledge to alter the interpretation of earlier knowledge without erasing the earlier state itself.

> Resolution may propagate across time, while lived understanding catches up locally.

The curator's duty is to preserve the structure required for that reconciliation to remain possible.
